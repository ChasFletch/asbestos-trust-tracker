# Vanderbilt Minerals disclosure-statement proceedings — key filings before October 19, 2026

**Research status:** Internal primary-source brief; not a published article, legal advice, tracker update, or a finding on the merits of any objection.  
**Case:** *In re Vanderbilt Minerals, LLC* (now referred to as Syracuse Minerals, LLC in the October 13 reply), No. 26-60110 (WAK), U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Northern District of New York.  
**Source cutoff:** October 14, 2026.  
**Method:** Reviewed the cited case-agent docket documents directly. Statements below describe what parties filed or argued; they are not determinations by the Bankruptcy Court.

## Procedural posture

The Disclosure Statement Hearing is set for **October 19, 2026, at 11:00 a.m. Eastern** before Chief Judge Wendy A. Kinsella. The notice sets **December 14, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. Eastern** for the confirmation hearing. The court had directed the debtor to file a revised proposed disclosure-statement order and trust distribution procedures (TDPs) by September 25, and objections were due October 6. [Docket 970, filed September 18](https://www.veritaglobal.net/Vanderbilt/document/2660110260918000000000004).

The debtor's original disclosure-statement motion, Docket 859, asks the court to approve the disclosure statement, solicitation and voting procedures, and a confirmation process. [Docket 859, filed August 6](https://www.veritaglobal.net/vanderbilt/document/2660110260806000000000003). The amended plan and amended disclosure statement are Docket Nos. 962 and 963, referenced in the hearing notice. The debtor filed the Vanderbilt Liquidation Trust Distribution Procedures on September 25 as Docket 990, attaching them as Exhibit A; the notice says the TDPs set procedures for resolving talc claims under the plan and trust agreement and reserves the debtor's right to revise or supplement them. [Docket 990](https://www.veritaglobal.net/vanderbilt/document/2660110260925000000000002).

No order resolving the objections was located in the reviewed docket materials. The October 19 hearing is therefore the next identified decision point, rather than evidence that the disclosure statement or a trust has been approved.

## Key filings and objections

### 1. U.S. Trustee objection — Docket 1012, filed October 6

[Primary document](https://www.veritaglobal.net/vanderbilt/document/2660110261006000000000001)

The U.S. Trustee asks the court to **deny the disclosure-statement motion and decline to approve the disclosure statement as presented**. The filing’s main positions are:

- The disclosure statement allegedly omits or inadequately explains talc-claim recovery estimates, timing, the liquidation analysis, the TDPs, conditions to effectiveness, and the impact of the pending settlement-order appeal.
- It says Class 5 talc-claim estimates and recoveries are shown as “Unknown,” despite disclosure of $11 million of distributable cash and stated available insurance limits; the objection argues creditors need a range, assumptions, timing, and first-distribution/source explanation.
- It challenges disclosure and legal support for the proposed insurance-entity injunction, releases, and exculpation, including the identities of protected parties, consideration, claims released, and possible effect of reversal of the global-settlement order.
- It argues a plan term cannot bind the district court or non-party appellees in the settlement appeal and that consummation before appellate resolution may create equitable-mootness risk.
- It also raises the proposed treatment of Class 4 and reserves rights to supplement after the plan supplement, liquidation analysis, and response to insurer objections are available.

### 2. R.T. Vanderbilt objection and reservation of rights — Docket 1016, filed October 6

[Primary document](https://www.veritaglobal.net/vanderbilt/document/2660110261006000000000005)

R.T. Vanderbilt requests denial of the motion. It argues that the amended plan conflicts with the settlement agreement and May letter agreement by, among other things:

- not retaining the independent manager and Katten to defend the settlement appeal;
- including terms it says limit or influence mootness arguments and predetermine post-appeal consequences;
- using remaining sale proceeds while allegedly exposing the estate to substantial administrative claims; and
- failing to disclose those asserted risks adequately.

It characterizes the plan as patently unconfirmable and not proposed in good faith, says it may seek an enforcement proceeding for specific performance and damages if the plan goes forward, and asks either for its proposed changes or for plan confirmation efforts to wait until the settlement appeal is resolved. The objection explicitly reserves further issues concerning the TDPs and best-interests analysis.

### 3. Insurers’ supplemental objection — Docket 1017, filed October 6

[Primary document](https://www.veritaglobal.net/vanderbilt/document/2660110261006000000000006)

The insurers incorporate earlier objections and focus on the September 25 TDP filing. They argue that the TDPs establish a claim-liquidation process without incorporating or describing requirements in the insurance policies and coverage-in-place agreements. Their requested disclosure would warn that noncompliance could delay, reduce, or eliminate insurance recoveries and lead to future coverage litigation. They request denial of disclosure-statement approval absent that disclosure.

This filing does **not** establish that insurance proceeds will be unavailable; it presents the insurers’ asserted risk and says those coverage issues remain for applicable-law or future coverage litigation.

### 4. VM Buyer limited objection and joinder — Docket 1025, filed October 7

[Primary document](https://www.veritaglobal.net/vanderbilt/document/2660110261007000000000008)

VM Buyer joins R.T. Vanderbilt’s objection and seeks denial of the motion. It argues that the plan does not require a fiduciary to defend the sale appeal or adequately disclose asserted litigation and administrative-claim risk from that position. It also objects to plan language it says could prejudice mootness arguments and notes it is discussing a protocol for data transfer to the liquidation trust, reserving related rights.

### 5. Debtor reply to R.T. Vanderbilt — Docket 1028, filed October 13

[Primary document](https://www.veritaglobal.net/vanderbilt/document/2660110261013000000000002)

Filed at the sole direction of the independent manager, the debtor asks the court to approve the disclosure statement and responds **only** to R.T. Vanderbilt’s objections. The debtor’s central positions are:

- the May letter agreement’s outside date expired July 1 without a timely agreed extension, so R.T. Vanderbilt could not unilaterally revive the obligation later;
- the agreement required the debtor to take actions, not guarantee an appellate outcome, and the debtor says it performed through July 1;
- the independent manager and Katten cannot be compelled to provide indefinite, unpaid personal services, but have offered to continue at a reduced rate if a plan reserve funds that role;
- the plan does not restrict R.T. Vanderbilt’s own mootness arguments; and
- these are confirmation issues, not a reason to withhold disclosure-statement approval.

The reply proposes that confirmation could include a plan modification reserving a modest, not-yet-specified portion of distributable value for the independent manager and Katten to continue defending the settlement appeal, with unused funds returning to the liquidation trust.

## What is verified, and what remains open

**Verified from primary filings:** the hearing schedule; filing dates and docket numbers; filing of the TDPs; the positions and requested relief stated by the U.S. Trustee, R.T. Vanderbilt, insurers, buyer, and debtor.

**Not established by this review:** whether the court will approve the disclosure statement, sustain any objection, require modifications, confirm a plan, form/open the liquidation trust, or set a payment percentage or claimant payment amount. The filings contain contested characterizations and proposed terms; they should not be reported as adjudicated facts.
