# Vi-Jon Files Chapter 11 to Channel Talc Claims into 524(g) Trust

**Vi-Jon, LLC**, the private-label dry bath products company behind Epsom salts and body powder sold at retailers including Amazon, Dollar General, and Walgreens, filed a voluntary Chapter 11 petition in the District of Delaware on August 2, 2026 (Case No. 26-11216). The filing is a prenegotiated 524(g) restructuring designed to channel **367 active talc personal injury claims** (356 mesothelioma, 11 lung cancer) into a trust.

## Key Terms

The trust would be funded by a **guaranteed $25 million cash contribution** from non-debtor affiliates (Emprise Group), plus assigned insurance rights under policies with approximately **$900 million in aggregate limits** (from solvent insurers), a $1 million promissory note, real estate sale proceeds, and a contingent $20 million settlement fee tied to a future sale of the parent at enterprise value above $1 billion.

The plan targets **confirmation at 110 days** and consummation at 120 days from the petition date — an aggressive timeline for a 524(g) case.

## Background

Vi-Jon permanently discontinued talc product sales in 2016. Claims surged approximately **400% since 2023**, driven by plaintiffs' counsel adding the debtor to pre-existing cases. Two adverse verdicts totaling approximately $20 million landed in May–July 2026 ($10.2M in *Heyer v. A.H. Bennett Co.* and $16.75M in *Ludwig v. Sumitomo*), against a company projecting only $300,000 in adjusted EBITDA for 2026.

A 2023 corporate restructuring separated the dry goods business from the broader enterprise (now Emprise Group/Nice-Pak), retaining only the Epsom salts/body powder operations and the talc liability at Vi-Jon. The NERA liability forecast increased from $59–102 million (nominal, 2023) to **$720 million** (nominal, May 2026) — a 7–12x revision.

## Significance for the Trust System

While Vi-Jon's claims are talc-related rather than asbestos, the case uses the Section 524(g) mechanism originally designed for asbestos trusts. This continues the trend of non-asbestos mass tort defendants seeking channeling injunctions under 524(g)'s framework. The case is before Judge Mary F. Walrath, with Sidley Austin as debtor's counsel and Caplin & Drysdale representing the ad hoc plaintiffs' group.

**No trust has been established yet.** The plan and disclosure statement are due approximately September 1, 2026.
