# Miyoshi Chapter 11 Case Closes; Confirmed Talc Trust Structure Remains Governed by the Plan

The U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas entered a **Final Decree on September 21, 2026** closing Miyoshi America, Inc.’s Chapter 11 case.[1] The decree closes the case effective on entry, while preserving the court’s plan-based jurisdiction and authorizing distributions consistent with the confirmed plan.[1]

This is a **talc personal-injury trust** case. It is not a newly added asbestos tracker trust, and this article does not add Miyoshi to the asbestos-trust count or change any asbestos-trust figure. The case is useful as a narrowly framed §524(g) procedural example: it shows why closing a Chapter 11 case is not the same thing as erasing the substantive rights and implementation framework established by a confirmed, effective plan.

## The timeline established by public case records

The public case-agent record states that Miyoshi filed its Chapter 11 petition on April 27, 2026; its prepackaged plan was confirmed June 12; the plan became effective June 26; and the final decree was entered September 21.[2]

| Public case milestone | Date | What the source supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chapter 11 petition | April 27, 2026 | Miyoshi commenced the Chapter 11 case. |
| Confirmation order | June 12, 2026 | The court confirmed Miyoshi’s prepackaged plan and approved the disclosure statement. |
| Effective-date notice | June 26, 2026 | The notice says the plan’s conditions were satisfied or waived and that the plan became effective and was substantially consummated. |
| Final decree | September 21, 2026 | The court closed the Chapter 11 case subject to the decree’s retained-jurisdiction and plan-preservation terms. |

## What the confirmed and effective plan says about the talc trust

The confirmation order states that the **Talc Personal Injury Trust** and the talc personal-injury channeling injunction would be established through the order and that, as of the plan’s effective date, the trust would assume specified liabilities relating to talc personal-injury claims.[3]

The effective-date notice further states that the Miyoshi Talc Personal Injury Trust would assume and be solely responsible for the debtor’s liabilities with respect to talc personal-injury claims, subject to the plan and confirmation order. It also says that a claimant release is a precondition to receiving a payment from that trust.[4]

Those are plan-and-notice statements about the established legal framework. They do **not** supply a current payment percentage, a current trust balance, a cumulative amount paid, a current claim-processing status, or an individual claimant result.[3] [4]

## What the final decree changes—and what it does not

The final decree closes the Chapter 11 case, directs a final post-confirmation quarterly report and fee payment, and retains jurisdiction over matters related to its implementation and enforcement.[1]

It also contains an explicit boundary: the decree says that it does **not** change the amount or nature of any distribution or other substantive right that a claim or interest would have had under the plan, confirmation order, Bankruptcy Code, or Bankruptcy Rules. It authorizes the reorganized debtor or another plan-authorized entity to make distributions consistent with the plan and confirmation order.[1]

> **Confirmed:** Miyoshi’s Chapter 11 case was closed by a September 21 final decree after a plan confirmation and a public June 26 effective-date notice. The public confirmation materials describe a talc personal-injury trust and channeling-injunction framework.
>
> **Not established by these documents:** A current payment percentage, a current trust balance, a cumulative payout, an open public filing portal, the outcome of any particular claim, or a conclusion that the decree itself resolved every implementation question.

## Why case closure is not a substitute for a trust notice

A final decree is a court order about the bankruptcy case’s status. It is not automatically a claimant-facing trust operations notice. For public research, the relevant source depends on the question:

| Research question | Source that should control |
| --- | --- |
| Whether the bankruptcy case was closed | Final decree and court/case-agent docket. |
| Whether the plan became effective | Effective-date notice and confirmed plan. |
| Trust legal structure and the channeling framework | Confirmed plan, trust agreement, and confirmation order. |
| Current claim-submission mechanics, payment terms, or a payment percentage | A later trust-administration notice, trust distribution procedures, or other controlling operational source. |

This source-by-source distinction prevents a case-closing order from being overstated as a new payment or claims-processing announcement.

## Scope note for the asbestos tracker

Miyoshi’s plan materials concern talc personal-injury claims. AsbestosTrusts.org’s public tracker remains limited to U.S. asbestos bankruptcy trusts. This article would therefore be published as a carefully labeled **research explainer**, not as a Miyoshi tracker record or a new aggregate count, asset, or payment figure.
