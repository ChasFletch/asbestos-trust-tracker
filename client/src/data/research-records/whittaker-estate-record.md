# Whittaker, Clark & Daniels, Inc. — bankruptcy estate

**Bankruptcy estate / no operating trust — status checked October 26, 2026.** Whittaker, Clark & Daniels, Inc. and its affiliated debtors remain a Chapter 11 bankruptcy record, not an operating asbestos-trust record. As of this check, the reviewed official public record establishes no confirmed plan, operating Section 524(g) trust, trust payment percentage, or trust claims-filing process. The estate settlement approved in the Bankruptcy Court's October 22, 2026 opinion and the proposed trust provisions described below are not operative claimant recoveries. [2] [6] [12]

## Estate details

The debtors filed Chapter 11 on **April 26, 2023**, in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of New Jersey. The cases are jointly administered under **23-13575 (MBK)** before Judge Michael B. Kaplan. The official case-agent page identifies the four cases as follows: [2]

- **Whittaker, Clark & Daniels, Inc.** — 23-13575.
- **Brilliant National Services, Inc.** — 23-13576.
- **L.A. Terminals, Inc.** — 23-13581.
- **Soco West, Inc.** — 23-13578.

The Third Circuit describes the debtors’ historical business as including production, storage, and distribution of asbestos-containing talc. It reports that **roughly 2,700 plaintiffs had asserted asbestos-related injury claims before bankruptcy**. That is a historical plaintiff count, not a verified count of 2,700 separate lawsuits, current pending cases, or claims accepted by a trust. [1] (pp. 7–11)

## What the Third Circuit decided

In its amended opinion filed **April 27, 2026**, *In re Whittaker Clark & Daniels Inc.*, 176 F.4th 241, the Third Circuit affirmed two distinct conclusions. First, Whittaker’s board validly authorized its Chapter 11 filing notwithstanding the South Carolina receiver’s appointment. Second, the product-line successor-liability claims at issue against Brenntag belong to the debtors’ bankruptcy estates under **11 U.S.C. § 541(a)(1)**. [1] (pp. 18, 31, 53) [9] (“Opinions Below,” p. 2)

The transaction background matters. In 2004, Whittaker, Brilliant, and Soco sold substantially all their operating assets to certain subsidiaries of Brenntag North America. The agreements expressly excluded pre-sale asbestos and environmental liabilities. The appellate ownership ruling concerns the successor-liability theories asserted despite that contractual exclusion; the exclusion is not a determination that the buyer has no possible liability. [1] (pp. 9, 14–17, 45–53)

The April 27, 2026 opinion is the amended opinion, not the September 10, 2025 version. The accompanying rehearing order granted panel rehearing, directed filing of the amended opinions, denied rehearing en banc, and left the appellate judgments unchanged. [10] (p. 2)

### Why estate ownership matters

**The appellate holding places these product-line claims within the estate’s control**, rather than allowing individual plaintiffs to pursue or settle them independently in separate suits against the buyer. The court explains that the estate representative has authority over estate-owned causes of action and that creditors cannot separately prosecute, settle, or release them unless the estate relinquishes that authority. [1] (pp. 33–34, 51–53)

This changes the route for any recovery on those theories. Proceeds would be administered through the bankruptcy estates and the applicable distribution structure, rather than reserved to an individual plaintiff who obtains a separate successor-liability judgment. If a later confirmed and effective plan creates and funds a tort-claims trust, the applicable plan and trust documents would govern that later route. **No such operating trust route is established as of October 26, 2026.** Neither the ownership ruling nor the estate settlement establishes any person’s payment amount. [1] (p. 34 n. 15, pp. 51–53) [6] (§1.143 and Article IV.D–F)

The holding should not be expanded into a statement that every claim against Brenntag is estate property. The court distinguishes claims based on a particularized injury directly traceable to a defendant’s own conduct from claims based on harm to the debtor that produces secondary harm to creditors. This record addresses the product-line claims decided in this case, not an assessment of any individual’s separate rights. [1] (pp. 45, 51–53)

## Estate settlement — approved in an October 22, 2026 opinion; order to follow

**On October 22, 2026, the Bankruptcy Court granted the motion to approve the $535 million Estate Claims Settlement in a memorandum opinion, Docket 3013: “the Settlement Motion is GRANTED.” As of the October 26, 2026 docket check, no order on the settlement had been entered; the newest entries were monthly fee statements.** The debtors’ September 3, 2024 motion, Docket 1297, sought approval of a settlement with the parties the opinion calls the “Settling Non-Debtors”: the Brenntag entities, National Indemnity Company (NICO), and DBUS. The amount is settlement consideration for the debtors’ estates. It is **not a trust balance, an available trust fund, or an individual claimant payout**. [3] (pp. 2–3, 18; Exhibit A)

The payment structure also limits what the headline amount means. The filed summary counts debtor-in-possession financing toward the settlement contribution, with the later contribution reduced by cash loan principal already funded. Its settlement effective date requires both the settlement order and summary-judgment order to become final, so the October 22 opinion is not itself that effective date. The July 14, 2026 Committee filing similarly notes that financing reduces the remaining proposed contribution. No calculation of a current distributable balance or trust allocation is made here. [3] (Exhibit A, “Effective Date” and “Settlement Payment”) [4] (¶¶2–3)

Before the opinion, the **July 14, 2026** Committee filing stated that the settlement “remains contested and sub judice,” meaning still before the court for decision, and an **August 7, 2026** Committee objection described court approval of the estate settlement as a condition. The October 22 opinion resolved that motion. The October 26 public-docket check is not a certification of every sealed, restricted, delayed, or unindexed record. [4] (¶¶2–4) [5] (¶¶51–52) [12] [13]

The Third Circuit’s April 27, 2026 opinion did **not** approve the settlement. It expressly described the settlement as subject to Bankruptcy Court approval. Nor does the Bankruptcy Court's October 22 approval prove that a plan has been confirmed or a trust is operating: the proposed plan separately states that the estate settlement is not contingent on plan confirmation. [1] (pp. 13–14) [6] (Article IV.D)

## Supreme Court petitions — two different procedural positions

**As of October 26, 2026, both petitions remain pending on the retrieved official public Supreme Court dockets; neither docket shows a disposition.** Their issues and response histories differ. [7] [8]

**No. 26-159 — Peter Protopapas.** The receiver’s petition was filed July 24, 2026 and docketed August 3. It seeks reconsideration of the bankruptcy/receivership issue in light of another Supreme Court decision. Respondents’ brief in opposition was **filed August 25, 2026**, and the petitioner’s reply was **filed September 4, 2026**, still the latest entry shown at the October 26 check. **November 6 is not a pending opposition deadline for this petition.** [7] [9] (Question Presented)

**No. 26-263 — Official Committee of Talc Claimants.** The petition was filed August 25, 2026 and docketed September 8. It asks whether a creditor’s claim against a nondebtor can be estate property under §541(a)(1) when the debtor could not have asserted it before bankruptcy under governing state law. The September 14 docket entry extends the response deadline to **November 6, 2026, for all respondents**. The latest entry date shown at the October 26 check is October 8, for amicus briefs; no opposition brief or disposition appears in the retrieved docket. The petition’s question is not a Supreme Court holding. [8] [11] (Question Presented)
