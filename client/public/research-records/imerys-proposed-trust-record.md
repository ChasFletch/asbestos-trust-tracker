## Bankruptcy background and proposed structure

The Imerys debtors entered Chapter 11 amid thousands of talc personal-injury claims. The Third Circuit’s 2022 account describes the company’s inability to continue defending the growing claims and its goal of channeling them into a Section 524(g) trust. The October 9, 2026 opinion also describes Imerys’s distribution of talc to Johnson & Johnson. These are bankruptcy-background facts, not findings that any particular claimant’s injury was caused by asbestos-contaminated talc. [16] [12]

The January 20, 2026 proposed plan addresses talc-related claims and future demands through a trust and proposed channeling injunction. Under §4.6.1, the trust would assume specified liabilities of the debtors and other defined Protected Parties **on the Effective Date**. The coordinated Cyprus plan participates in the shared trust structure. The protected-party definitions and schedules, rather than a blanket reference to all affiliates, control the proposed scope. [2] [3]

The located plan defines “Talc Personal Injury Claim” to exclude “Foreign Claims.” Its proposed assumption clause likewise excludes Foreign Claims except for obligations expressly assumed under the plan. That is a description of filed definitions, not a determination of anyone’s rights or claim classification. The introduction expressly says the plan documents are not admissions of the existence, merits, or amount of liability, subject to the plan’s stated exceptions. [2]

Johnson & Johnson is a settlement party here, not one of the three Imerys debtors identified above. J&J-related bankruptcy proceedings are outside this record; they must not be treated as the Imerys case. The filed J&J agreement itself distinguishes “J&J Related Bankruptcy Case” arrangements. [1] [7]

## Proposed funding components — never summed

**Document basis:** January 20, 2026 filed plan package, recovered October 14, 2026. All monetary amounts below are **U.S. dollars**. They describe proposed contributions, contractual payment obligations, or note principal—not present trust cash, audited assets, or amounts actually transferred. Applicable trigger dates and effective-date conditions remain controlling. No total is calculated or displayed.

**Imerys Settlement Funds:** The plan defines **$75 million**, plus Sale Proceeds, less **$376,630.14** of specified Imerys S.A. DIP-document negotiation and preparation costs. Only Sale Proceeds remaining after case funding and plan-payable or reserved amounts would reach the trust. No final net Sale-Proceeds amount is supplied in this definition. [2]

**Rio Tinto cash contribution:** **$80 million**. The attached agreement ties payment to the Rio Tinto/Zurich Trigger Date. Separate assigned credits and future credits are also contemplated; they have no verified dollar valuation in this record. [2] [5]

**Zurich cash contribution:** **$260 million**, separately defined in the plan. It is not merged with Rio Tinto’s contribution or with insurance-rights valuations. [2]

**Cyprus Contributions:** The Cyprus plan includes a Plan Note, pledge agreement, Freeport guarantee, and Assignment. The Plan Note would be an **unsecured seven-year promissory note with $195 million outstanding principal as of the Effective Date**. This is neither an immediate cash contribution nor proof of payment. Assigned rights are not given a nominal value here. [3]

**XL cash contribution:** **$28.25 million**. A separate California Fire Claim contribution includes **$1 million for the trust**. The same definition specifies **$1.25 million for Imerys Filtration Minerals, Inc., a non-debtor**, not for the trust; that non-trust amount must not be included in a trust-assets field. These amounts remain separate. [2] [6]

**J&J Initial Payment:** **$225 million**, separately defined in the attached agreement. Receipt and allocation to the trust or designated accounts are governed by that agreement; this record does not establish the amount actually received. [7]

**J&J Insurance Payments:** A separate **$280 million contractual obligation**, defined by reference to insurance recoveries and a backstop provision. It is not combined with the Initial Payment. The agreement’s escrow and designated-account provisions mean the contractual amount cannot be treated as current cash held by an operating trust. No payment-completion finding is made as of October 14, 2026. [7]

**Other proposed assets:** The plan lists Home Proceeds, qualified-settlement-fund amounts, debtor cash outside reserves, non-cash contributions, insurance interests, causes of action, debtor stock, and residual reserve cash. Their current transferable values are not established here. Some assets and proceeds may pass through accounts or funds before transfer; they must not be counted again under another label. [2]

## Proposed claimant treatment, not an operative process

**As checked October 14, 2026**, the January 20 filed Trust Distribution Procedures remain proposal evidence in this record, not a live filing guide. They describe separate **Fund A** for specified ovarian-cancer categories and **Fund B** for mesothelioma, secondary mesothelioma, and lung-cancer categories. The different funding-source allocations are internal proposed account allocations, **not claimant payment percentages**. This page supplies no filing instructions, eligibility determination, scheduled claim value, or recovery estimate. [4]

The plan separately defines confirmation and effectiveness. Its effectiveness provisions contemplate confirmation and district-court affirmation for both the Imerys and Cyprus plans, together with other conditions. A creditor vote, a proposed confirmation order, or a sale-appeal decision cannot substitute for proof of those milestones. No completed effective-date or operational milestone was verified in this October 14 check. [2] [18]
