# DBMP Claimants Ask Supreme Court to Review Texas Two-Step Bankruptcy

A petition filed **September 17, 2026** asks the U.S. Supreme Court to review a Fourth Circuit decision involving **DBMP, LLC** and the use of a Texas divisional merger in an asbestos bankruptcy.[1] The Supreme Court docket lists the matter as **No. 26-394, *Herlihy v. DBMP, LLC*** and states that a response is due October 22, 2026.[2]

The filing is a **petition for certiorari**—a request that the Court review a lower-court decision. It is **not** a grant of review, a Supreme Court merits ruling, a change to an existing asbestos trust’s payment percentage, or a new trust-funding announcement.

## What the public filing confirms

The petition identifies Michael Herlihy, Ann Herlihy, and the Estate of Peter L. Bergrud as petitioners and DBMP, LLC as respondent. It arises from the DBMP Chapter 11 case and related district-court and Fourth Circuit proceedings listed in the filing.[1]

The question presented asks whether a debtor’s pursuit of bankruptcy as part of a Texas Two-Step arrangement lacks good faith such that there is “cause” to lift the automatic stay under **11 U.S.C. § 362(d)**.[1]

## Why the §524(g) context matters

The petition describes CertainTeed’s divisional-merger transaction and DBMP’s Chapter 11 case as part of a process that sought to establish a trust under **11 U.S.C. § 524(g)** for present and future asbestos claims.[1] It also says the claimants sought relief from the automatic stay.

Those statements provide the procedural context for the petition. They should not be read as a current trust-administration notice. The filing does not provide an operational trust payment percentage, a current trust balance, a claims portal, a claimant payment result, or a Supreme Court ruling on the merits.[1] [2]

## What has not happened

The Supreme Court’s public docket, as reviewed for this draft, does **not** show that certiorari has been granted or denied. It lists the September 17 petition and an October 22 response deadline.[2]

> **Confirmed:** A certiorari petition was filed in Supreme Court No. 26-394, and it presents a stated question about good faith and relief from the automatic stay in the context of the DBMP Texas Two-Step bankruptcy.
>
> **Not established:** A grant of review, a merits decision, any change to DBMP’s bankruptcy plan or proposed trust structure, a current payment percentage, a trust balance, or an individual claim outcome.
